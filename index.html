Class & Lab Structure

Theory classes run Monday–Friday. Labs are scheduled on fixed days with practical sessions. Laptops are allowed in class — but phones must be on silent and kept away during lectures. Labs are open from 7 AM to 10:30 PM daily.

📋

Attendance Rules

Minimum 75% attendance is mandatory per subject. Fall below this and you'll be detained from that subject's exam. Track attendance daily on aums.amrita.edu — don't let it sneak up on you.

🎓

CGPA Tips

  • Never miss lab record submissions
  • Assignments are easy marks — do them seriously
  • Start MIS prep 2 weeks before, not 2 days
  • Ask doubts early — faculty are approachable
  • Form study groups for tough subjects

Clubs & Extracurriculars

Amrita Amaravati has a thriving club culture. Joining a club in your first semester is one of the best decisions you can make — it builds your network, resume, and sanity.

Technical

RELU

The AI/ML club of the campus. Deep dives into machine learning, neural networks, and data science projects. Perfect for CSE-AI and AI&DS students.

Technical · Event Organizer

Chakravyuha

The flagship technical fest club. Organizes campus-wide competitions including this very event! Great exposure to event management and leadership.

Technical

Robotics Club

Build, program and compete with robots. From line followers to autonomous bots — hands-on engineering that textbooks can't teach.

Cultural · Dance

Nrityasparsha

The premier dance club of Amrita Amaravati. Classical and contemporary dance forms, intra and inter-college competitions, and cultural events.

Creative · Fine Arts

Advika

For the artists on campus. Painting, sketching, and visual arts workshops. Showcases work at campus fests and helps you express your creative side.

Creative · Photography

Drusya

The digital camera and photography club. Learn composition, editing, event photography — and get to shoot all the major campus events!

Sports

Avisrutha

The sports club of Amrita Amaravati. Volleyball, basketball, badminton, cricket, and more. Inter-campus tournaments and intra-college competitions throughout the year.

Pro Tip

Why Join a Club?

Clubs give you internship opportunities, leadership roles, and connections that classrooms don't. First-year recruitment drives happen in August–September — be ready.

How to Join a Club

1

Attend the Club Expo

Held in the first 2–4 weeks of the semester. All clubs set up stalls — explore, talk to seniors, and shortlist 2–3 you like.

2

Fill the Interest Form

Each club has a Google Form or sign-up sheet. Fill it immediately — slots fill fast, especially for popular clubs like Robotics and RELU.

3

Attend the Recruitment Drive

Most technical clubs have a small task or interview. Don't stress — they care more about enthusiasm than skill at the fresher level.

4

Show Up Consistently

Attendance at club meetings matters. Be regular in the first month and you'll naturally get more responsibilities and opportunities.

Wake up, freshen up. Mess opens early — breakfast is better when you're not rushing.

8:00 AM

First class begins. Be there 5 minutes early — punctuality is noticed by professors.

Section 05

The Fresher's Survival Kit

✅ DO THIS

  • Register on AUMS on Day 1 and bookmark it
  • Attend the first week of every class — it sets attendance baseline
  • Make friends with seniors from your branch ASAP
  • Keep a physical notebook for important deadlines
  • Join at least one technical and one non-technical club
  • Save professor contact numbers (most share on first day)
  • Explore the campus fully in your first week
  • Submit assignments before the deadline, not on it

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Bunking classes in the first month — attendance deficit is hard to recover
  • Ignoring lab record submissions — they carry marks
  • Pulling all-nighters before every exam — it doesn't work long-term
  • Comparing yourself to others constantly — everyone has their own pace
  • Forgetting to eat regularly — affects focus and health
  • Keeping your phone on during lectures — professors do notice
  • Waiting till 10 PM to start assignments due at midnight
  • Not asking for help when you're lost — everyone is lost at first

🚨 Common Fresher Mistakes

The Attendance Trap

Thinking "75% is easy." Do the math — miss 2 days per week and you're already in danger zone by mid-semester.

The Procrastination Loop

Delaying assignments till the last hour. In Amrita, multiple deadlines often cluster together. Start early, always.

The Isolation Phase

Staying in the room because campus feels overwhelming. Push yourself to talk to people. It gets much easier after Week 2.

The CGPA Shock

First-semester marks are often a surprise. Don't panic — understand the marking scheme and adjust your strategy fast.

Ignoring Labs

Treating labs as unimportant. Lab exams are 20 marks. Record files, viva prep, and practical execution — all matter.

No Senior Network

Not befriending seniors. They have previous year question papers, insider tips, and can warn you about tough professors.

⏰ Time Management Framework

MORNING BLOCK

Attend all classes. No skipping. This is non-negotiable in Semester 1.

AFTERNOON BLOCK

Labs + any pending assignment work. Use library resources if needed.

EVENING BLOCK

1 hour of club activity or sports. Physical activity keeps you sane.

NIGHT BLOCK

2 hours of study/revision. Prepare for the next day, review today's notes.

📝 Senior's MIS Exam Prep Plan (4-Week Strategy)

Week 1 (4 Weeks Out)

  • Collect all lecture notes and slides
  • Identify weak topics per subject
  • Ask seniors for previous question papers
  • Make a subject-wise checklist

Week 2 (3 Weeks Out)

  • Complete all pending assignments
  • Start studying from the syllabus end
  • Make short notes / flashcards
  • Form study groups for tough subjects

Week 3 (2 Weeks Out)

  • Practice previous year questions
  • Focus on repeated topics from past papers
  • Lab records must be fully updated
  • Sleep 7 hrs minimum — don't skip

Week 4 (Exam Week)

  • Revise short notes only — no new topics
  • Eat properly and stay hydrated
  • Reach exam hall 10 mins early
  • Answer what you know first, then tackle hard ones

Section 07

Fresher FAQs

The most common questions every Amrita Amaravati fresher Googles at 2 AM — answered.

What is AUMS and when should I register?+

AUMS (Amrita University Management System) is your one-stop academic portal for attendance, timetable, marks, assignments, and fee payments. Register on Day 1 using the credentials given during admission. Bookmark aums.amrita.edu and check it every single day — seriously.

What happens if my attendance drops below 75%?+

You'll be detained from appearing for that subject's end-semester exam. This is non-negotiable. If you're between 65–74%, you may petition the faculty with valid medical certificates — but don't count on it. Prevention is everything. Track it weekly on AUMS.

Is hostel mandatory for freshers?+

Yes. First-year students are required to stay in the hostel. This is standard for most batches. Day-scholar options are usually considered from second year onward (subject to policy — check with your admission coordinator). Hostel life is actually a great way to build your network fast.

How does the CGPA / grading system work?+

Amrita uses a 10-point grading scale: O=10, A+=9, A=8, B+=7, B=6, C=5. Your CGPA is calculated as the weighted average of grade points × credit hours for each subject. Aim for 8+ to keep options open for research positions and competitive internships.

Can I use my phone in class or during labs?+

Phones must be on silent and kept away during lectures and labs — this is the general rule across faculties. Laptops are allowed in class for note-taking (keep it on airplane mode if you're not using it for class material). Getting caught on your phone during class is a quick way to get noticed for the wrong reasons.

What's the MIS exam and how should I prepare?+

MIS (Mid Internal Semester) exams happen twice a semester and account for 30 marks each. They typically cover the topics taught in the preceding weeks. Start preparing 3–4 weeks before using previous question papers (ask seniors), short notes, and study groups. Do not wait until the week before.

When should I join clubs — Day 1 or later?+

The Club Expo happens in the first 2–4 weeks of the semester. This is your window. Most clubs recruit freshers only once a year, in August–September. Don't miss it thinking "I'll join next semester" — you won't get the same onboarding experience. Show up, talk to seniors, and apply to 2–3 clubs.

Is food at the mess and canteen decent?+

The mess serves three meals a day and is generally decent (your opinion will vary — most freshers take a month to adjust). The canteen is the popular alternative especially for evening snacks and late nights. Swiggy and Zomato also deliver to the hostel gates, so you won't go hungry.

Campus Map Guide

The Amrita Amaravati campus is 400+ acres — getting lost is a rite of passage. Here are the key spots every fresher needs to know.

🏛️

Main Academic Block

Theory classrooms, faculty offices, admin block. Most of your Week 1 orientation happens here.

💻

Computer Labs (CISCO)

High-end CISCO-powered labs, open 7 AM – 10:30 PM. Bring your student ID for after-hours access.

📚

Central Library

Multi-floor library with digital resources, reference books, and silent study spaces. Great for exam season.

🍽️

Central Mess & Canteen

Main mess for hostel students. Separate canteen for snacks and à la carte. Canteen is open late.

🏃

Avisrutha Sports Grounds

Cricket, volleyball, basketball, badminton courts. Managed by the sports club. Open evenings and weekends.

🏥

Health Centre

Campus medical facility for first aid and routine check-ups. Know where this is from Day 1 — emergency visits happen.

🏦

Dhanlaxmi Bank + ATM

On-campus banking and ATM. Essential for managing your monthly budget without leaving campus.

🏠

Hostel Blocks

Separate boys and girls blocks, Wi-Fi throughout. Your home for the next year — explore it in Week 1.

🛒

General Store

Basic necessities, stationery, toiletries, and snacks. Locate this on Day 1 — you'll be back daily.

💡 Pro tip: Walk the entire campus in your first week. Find the quickest route from your hostel block to your lecture hall and the lab. Knowing these shortcuts saves 10 minutes every morning — and those minutes matter when class starts at 8 AM.
